# Comparison pages: three-reviewer wording review

22 November 2026. Review only: no page was edited. Three reviewers each read the same 12 live pages in full and counted patterns across all 162 comparison, alternatives and best-of pages.

Pages read in full: the compare hub; Sociana vs Whova, vs Hubilo, vs Cvent; Cvent vs Whova; Eventbrite vs Townscript; Cvent pricing; event app vs WhatsApp group; Cvent alternatives; Hubilo alternatives; best event apps in India; best dealer meet apps.

## Verdict

| Reviewer | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Who the writing speaks to | The founder is mostly right. About 30 to 40% of paragraphs on comparison and alternatives pages, about 30% on best-of guides and about 50% on pricing pages describe how the facts were checked or defended, not what a buyer should choose. |
| 2. Search and AI answers | Facts are strong and dated, but titles and headings use phrases nobody types ("where each fits", "an honest comparison for conferences & summits", "Fair is fair"). Alternatives pages are the best written. The hub is a list of links with no answer. |
| 3. Sociana and sources | Pages are mostly fair, and Sociana is under-sold: its price, its 300+ events and its own-brand store app are missing or buried. Sources are cited heavily, almost all as links to competitor homepages. |

### Example rewrites (every fact kept)

**Definitions line, Sociana vs Whova**
- Before: "'Not listed' means we could not find it on the vendor's pages that day, not that it does not exist. 'Not verified' means the row was not checked page by page, so we make no claim either way."
- After: "'Not listed' means Whova's website does not mention it. If a row matters to you, ask Whova to confirm it in writing."

**Review themes, Sociana vs Whova FAQ**
- Before: "Review themes cited on 19 Oct 2026 include notification volume (Trustpilot, four reviews, 2.6 average; G2 comments via learn.g2.com), connectivity at low-coverage venues, quote-only pricing… Against that, Whova holds 4.8 on the US App Store and G2, so these are themes, not a verdict on the product."
- After: "Organisers who leave Whova mention too many notifications, patchy connectivity at venues with weak signal and quote-only pricing. Whova is also rated 4.8 on the US App Store and on G2, so many organisers are happy with it."

**Cost table, Cvent pricing**
- Before: a three-row table, each row "Not derivable from published pricing".
- After, one line: "Cvent does not publish licence or per-registration rates, so we cannot give a cost for 300, 1,000 or 3,000 attendees. Ask Cvent for a written quote at your attendee count, with every add-on listed."

**Disclosure, best dealer meet apps**
- Before: "Disclosure. Sociana publishes this guide and is listed once, at position 1 of 7, labelled as our product. We compiled it on 19 Oct 2026 from public vendor pages linked below, without hands-on testing, and nobody paid for a place."
- After: "Sociana is one of the seven apps listed, and we say so in the table. Details come from each vendor's own pages, not from trials, and prices were current on 19 Oct 2026."

### What should stay

One price-check date near each price or table; one ownership line near the top; the report-a-correction route; the "where Sociana is not the right pick" blocks and "Pick X when" lists; "ask for a written quote" tips; migration steps.

### Sample answer-first openings (facts from the pages only)

- **Sociana vs Whova:** "Pick Whova for conferences and academic or association meetings that want a proven, widely installed attendee app with abstract management; it holds 4.8 stars from about 253,000 US App Store ratings. Pick Sociana for hospitality-heavy meets needing per-guest flights, rooms and pickups, WhatsApp, and rupee quotes with GST. Checked 19 Oct 2026."
- **Cvent pricing:** "Cvent does not publish prices, so its cost at 300, 1,000 or 3,000 attendees cannot be worked out from public data. Its pricing page lists modules, two tiers (Professional with 15 user logins; Enterprise with API and SSO) and unpriced add-ons."
- **Cvent vs Whova:** "Cvent suits global enterprises running large, multi-country event portfolios with procurement and security reviews. Whova suits a single conference that wants a familiar attendee app. Neither publishes prices, so we cannot say which is cheaper."

### Question headings to use

- Sociana vs X: Is Sociana better than Whova? · Which is cheaper? · Does Whova work in India? · Where is Whova better than Sociana?
- X vs Y: Is Cvent cheaper than Whova? · Which is better for a conference? · Does either bill in rupees?
- Pricing: How much does Cvent cost? · Does Cvent publish its prices? · Does Cvent charge per registration?
- Alternatives: What is the best Cvent alternative in India? · Which alternative bills in rupees with GST? · When should I stay on Cvent?
- Best-of: Which event app charges in rupees? · Which Indian event app has published prices?

## 4. Sources and links

Measured across all 162 pages: 3,304 links to 80 other sites inside page content, on 151 pages. 1,967 are marked nofollow; 1,337 are not. Most-linked: whova.com 175, cvent.com 152, eventbrite.com 137, swapcard.com 122, zoho.com 118.

| Pattern | Count | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| A "source" link on every table row, to the same competitor homepage | 2,740 "source" links | Remove. One sources list per page |
| Inline "(source …)" after a claim | 1,080 | Replace with plain text: "(Cvent site, 19 Oct 2026)" |
| A bare domain as link text | 309 | Plain text: "Cvent pricing page (checked 19 Oct 2026)" |
| A competitor's brand name as link text (the most valuable kind) | 45, on the 13 best-of pages | Remove the link; keep the name as text |
| Homepage linked as proof that something is absent | 52 for cvent.com alone | No link; a homepage cannot prove absence |
| Links with no nofollow mark | 1,337 | Make nofollow the default for every outside link in page content |
| Neutral sources (Wikipedia, DataReportal, TechCrunch) | few | Keep, as nofollow |

**Proposed citation rule**
1. Name the source and the check date in plain text. No link by default.
2. One "Sources and dates" list at the bottom of each page.
3. Link only the exact page behind a price, fee or ownership claim a buyer might dispute, marked nofollow, with link text such as "pricing page, checked 19 Oct 2026".
4. Never use a competitor's name or product keyword as link text, and never link a competitor's homepage.
5. Keep dated evidence (a screenshot or archive copy) on file for anything not linked.

**The trade-off.** Nofollow is a hint to search engines, not a guarantee. Fewer links make a claim harder for a reader to check in one click, so the page's credibility then rests on the date and the correction route.

## 6. Where the reviewers disagree

| Question | Reviewer view | Suggested resolution |
|---|---|---|
| "Best" and "ranked" in titles | Reviewer 2 wants "Best event apps in India… ranked" because buyers search it. Reviewer 3 says a self-ranked "best" is the biggest trust and legal risk | Keep "best" in the title because it is the searched word, but present the list by situation, not by numbered rank |
| Source links on figures | Reviewer 1 would keep a link on each figure. Reviewer 3 would remove nearly all | Follow the citation rule above: one nofollow link per disputed price or ownership claim, plain text for the rest |
| Describing Sociana in openings | Reviewer 2's sample opening calls Sociana "the usual alternative" | Do not use it: it is not a fact on the page. Use "one option" |

## 7. One set of rules for an editor

1. Write to "you", the buyer. If a sentence is about how the page was made, cut it.
2. Title: intent word (vs, alternatives, pricing, best) plus India plus year, 60 characters or fewer.
3. First 40 to 60 words: the verdict and two facts, readable on their own.
4. Headings are the questions buyers type.
5. One check date per table and per price; one ownership line per page.
6. Every "not verified / could not find" becomes "Not published: ask the vendor", or is deleted.
7. Remove tables and rows that carry no information.
8. Sociana: the job it does, one proof, the from-price wherever a rival's price is shown, and limits stated once without apology. No superlatives.
9. Sources in plain text with a date; links only as the citation rule allows.
10. Compare like with like; no unverified prices and no tiny-sample negatives about a competitor.

## Limits of this review

- 12 of 162 pages were read in full; the rest were only searched for patterns, so page-specific problems elsewhere will have been missed.
- Counts are of pages containing a phrase, taken from the text of the live pages on 22 November 2026.
- No competitor fact was re-checked against its source. The legal read is still needed.
- The risk notes are an editor's view, not legal advice.
